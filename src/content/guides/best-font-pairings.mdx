SaaS (Software as a Service) companies need to convey trust, modernity, and clarity. The typography should get out of the way of the product while still maintaining a strong brand presence.

## The "Tech Default"

**Inter + Inter**

You cannot talk about SaaS typography without mentioning Inter. Designed specifically for user interfaces, it's highly legible at small sizes. Pairing different weights of Inter (e.g., Black for headings, Regular for body) creates a monochromatic, clean, and highly technical look.

*   *Why it works:* Zero distraction. Pure functionality.
*   *Vibe:* Minimalist, startup, developer-friendly.

## The Modern Geometric

**Manrope + Manrope** (or Manrope + Inter)

Manrope is a crossover of different font types: it is semi-condensed, semi-rounded, semi-geometric, semi-din, semi-grotesque. It brings a lot of personality to headings while remaining readable.

*   *Why it works:* It feels friendlier and more distinct than standard neo-grotesques without losing the "tech" edge.
*   *Vibe:* Approachable, modern, design-focused.

## The "Premium Software" Look

**Playfair Display + Source Sans 3**

Not all SaaS is purely technical. Tools aimed at creative professionals, luxury markets, or high-end B2B often benefit from a more editorial approach.

*   *Why it works:* The high-contrast serif establishes authority and elegance, while the sans-serif body copy ensures technical information remains highly readable.
*   *Vibe:* Expensive, established, editorial.

## The Friendly Helper

**Poppins + Lora**

For B2C SaaS or tools focused on productivity and personal use, a softer approach is often better. Poppins has geometric, rounded forms that feel very welcoming.

*   *Why it works:* It removes the "coldness" of traditional tech fonts.
*   *Vibe:* Helpful, consumer-friendly, modern.
