# What is a Serif Font?

A serif font is characterized by small, decorative strokes or lines (called "serifs") that extend from the ends of the main strokes of its letters. These traditional typefaces are often associated with authority, history, elegance, and formal communication, making them a staple in print design.

## How Does It Work?

Serifs work by guiding the reader's eye horizontally across a line of text. The small structural extensions create a subtle baseline that physically links the letters together, which significantly enhances readability in dense, long-form text blocks, especially in printed materials.

## Real-World Example in Industry

Books, newspapers, and academic journals almost exclusively use serif fonts (like Times New Roman, Garamond, or Georgia) for their main body text because the serifs make reading long passages much less fatiguing on the eyes compared to sans-serif fonts.
