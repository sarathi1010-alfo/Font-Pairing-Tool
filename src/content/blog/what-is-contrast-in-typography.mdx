# What is Contrast in Typography?

Contrast in typography refers to the deliberate visual differences created between two or more typefaces, or between different elements of a single typeface. It is the fundamental principle used to establish visual hierarchy, emphasize important information, and make a design more dynamic and engaging for the reader. If you want to understand how these dynamics influence users, reading about the [psychology of font pairing](/blog/typography-psychology-guide) is highly recommended.

## How Does It Work?

Contrast is achieved through variations in weight (light vs. bold), size (large headings vs. small body text), style (italic vs. roman), and classification (serif vs. sans-serif). High contrast draws immediate attention, while low contrast creates a more subtle, unified aesthetic.

## Real-World Example in Industry

Magazine layouts often rely heavily on typographic contrast. A massive, ultra-bold, sans-serif headline placed immediately above a small, light, serif body paragraph creates a dramatic tension that immediately captures the reader's interest and clearly signals where to begin reading.
